THE BUSINESS PROBLEM

How do you build a realistic retail dataset when the data doesn't already exist?

Retail analytics depends on data that reflects how a real business actually operates. A simple random dataset would not capture the relationships between customers, products, branches, payments, seasonality and purchasing behaviour.

The objective was therefore to design a retail data environment where those relationships were explicitly defined and then translated into interconnected synthetic data.

Project disclaimer

JRAD Retail is a fictional and simulated retail environment created for portfolio and analytical purposes. The project is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or based on proprietary data from any real retailer.

The business scenarios and operational behaviours were informed by real-world observations of Nigerian superstore environments and translated into custom business rules, probability distributions and simulation logic.

PROJECT SCOPE

A connected retail ecosystem, not just a transaction table.

The system was designed to represent multiple dimensions of retail operations and customer behaviour.

Customer Data

Customer identities, demographics, registration, loyalty behaviour and acquisition channels.

Product Data

Product master information supporting purchasing, pricing, demand and downstream transaction logic.

Sales Transactions

Large-scale receipt and transaction-line data generated from interconnected business rules.

Branch Behaviour

Branch-level activity and customer distribution designed to create realistic operational variation.

Business Events

Seasonal and event-driven effects influencing traffic, spending, demand and peak shopping periods.

Operational Data

Loyalty, payments, customer experience, queues and pricing history supporting broader analytics.

THE BUILD

Designing the inputs before generating the transactions.

Seven artifacts, in the order they were built. Each one feeds the next — keep scrolling and they stack into the finished dataset.

ARTIFACT 01

Product Master

The commercial foundation of the ecosystem: every product the simulated business sells, with attributes that influence purchasing, pricing, demand, storage and downstream transaction behaviour.

  • 525 products / SKUs
  • 16 attributes each, from base price to inflation sensitivity
ARTIFACT 02

Distribution Library

Probability distributions and business rules for customer and transaction behaviour: branch-level customer distribution, age, demographics, registration, loyalty, payment methods, basket sizes and acquisition channels.

  • 5 branches
  • 800 registered customers
  • 10+ distribution sheets
ARTIFACT 03

Nigerian Identity Library

Structured pools of first names, surnames and gender-specific names, grouped by ethnicity, so generated customers carry realistic Nigerian identities.

  • 840 names
  • 18 name sheets
  • 6 groups: Yoruba, Igbo, Edo, Efik/Ibibio, Urhobo/Isoko, Others
ARTIFACT 04

Customer Master

Identity data, demographic distributions, branch logic, registration, loyalty and acquisition rules combined into structured customer records.

  • 16 fields per customer
  • IDs like CUS-BR001-000001 encode the branch
ARTIFACT 05

Business Events & Seasonality

Simulated events that shift customer traffic, basket size, spending, product demand and peak shopping hours across the year.

  • 10 events across 2026
  • 3 multipliers: traffic, basket, spend
  • up to 1.6× traffic in the Christmas season
ARTIFACT 06

Transaction Generation Engine

A custom Python engine that applies the business rules, customer behaviour, branch activity, product demand and pricing logic to simulate a full year of trading.

  • 20+ Python modules
  • 5 input workbooks, 1 transactions file out
ARTIFACT 07

Large-Scale Dataset Generation

The finished run: a large-scale simulated retail dataset, ready for analysis in SQL, Excel and Power BI.

  • 225,499 receipts
  • 1,807,529 transaction lines
  • 225,499 payments
