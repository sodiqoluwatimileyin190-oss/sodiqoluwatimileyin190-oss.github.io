01 — PROJECT OVERVIEW

Understanding who converts.

The analysis examines customer characteristics, banking relationships and demographic segments associated with personal loan acceptance.

Business Problem

THERA Bank's personal loan campaign recorded a relatively low overall acceptance rate. The objective was to determine which customer profiles were more likely to accept a personal loan offer and translate those patterns into actionable targeting recommendations.

Analysis Focus

  • Personal loan acceptance
  • Income & education segments
  • Age & family characteristics
  • Banking relationships
  • Mortgage ownership
02 — WHO SAID YES

From 9.6% to nearly 80%.

Follow the campaign one filter at a time. Every dot is a customer, and the bright ones accepted the loan.

01 / 04
  1. THE CAMPAIGN

    480 of 5,000 said yes.

    Only 9.6% of customers accepted the personal loan offer, which left plenty of room for sharper targeting.

  2. INCOME

    Income is the first filter.

    Out of every 100 high-income customers, about 36 accepted (35.9%). Among middle-income customers it was about 2 (2.1%), and low-income customers recorded virtually no acceptance.

  3. EDUCATION

    Education sharpens it.

    Professional (13.7%) and Graduate (13.0%) customers accepted at roughly three times the Undergraduate rate (4.4%).

  4. THE TARGET

    Together, they find the buyers.

    High-income Graduates accepted at 79.2% and high-income Professionals at 77.6%, more than eight times the campaign's overall rate.

03 — POWER BI DASHBOARD

From customer behavior to targeting strategy.

The dashboard is structured across three analytical views: executive overview, customer behavior and strategic recommendations.

DASHBOARD 01

Executive Overview

High-level view of customer volume, loan acceptance, income segments, age groups and education-level performance.

  • 4,520 of 5,000 customers turned the offer down.
  • Age barely moves acceptance: 9.4% to 10.6% across age groups.
  • High-income customers accepted at 35.9%, middle-income at 2.1%.
DASHBOARD 02

Customer Behavior & Banking Relationships

Examines family size, professional experience, banking services, credit card ownership and mortgage relationships.

  • Families of three accepted most often (13.2%); single-person households least (7.3%).
  • Securities account holders accepted at 11.5%, versus 9.4% without one.
  • Credit cards, online banking and experience level barely moved acceptance.
DASHBOARD 03

Strategic Recommendations & Targeting

Converts the analytical findings into actionable customer profiles and campaign targeting recommendations.

  • Acceptors averaged $144.75K in income versus $66.24K, and more than double the credit card spending.
  • High-income Graduates (79.2%) and Professionals (77.6%) are the standout segments.
  • ZIP code 94720 produced the most accepted loans (19), followed by 94305 (13).
04 — KEY FINDINGS

What the analysis revealed.

The dashboard highlights several customer characteristics associated with stronger loan acceptance.

9.6%

Acceptance was highly concentrated

Only 9.6% of the 5,000 customers accepted the personal loan offer, indicating substantial room for more precise campaign targeting.

13.7% vs 4.4%

Education mattered

Professional and graduate customers recorded considerably stronger acceptance rates than undergraduate customers.

35.9% vs 2.1%

Higher income was a major signal

Loan acceptance was heavily concentrated among higher-income customers, making income an important variable for future campaign prioritization.

05 — RECOMMENDED TARGET PROFILE

Who the next campaign should reach first.

  • High-income customers
  • Graduate or Professional education
  • Family size of 3–4
  • High mortgage commitments
  • Securities account holders

These customer segments consistently recorded the highest loan acceptance rates across income, education, family size and banking relationship analyses. Targeting these profiles is expected to maximize campaign conversion rates and improve marketing ROI.

79.2% acceptance among high-income Graduates versus 9.6% across the whole campaign
